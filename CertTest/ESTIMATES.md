## Conclusions

1. **Extra axioms are not needed for speed.** `native_decide` is only ≈ 2× faster than the kernel
   on the same computation; the kernel's GMP arithmetic makes M2 fast.
2. **The bottleneck of M1 is not arithmetic but proof-term glue per rectangle.** With 41,633 boxes,
   M1 is feasible but slow (hours to a day of CPU; a CI run of several hours).
3. **Recommended: M2**, a small verified rational interval checker (expression language with
   +, −, ×, ÷, integer powers, monotone-function rules, and table lookups for $J$, $J'$, powers),
   whose soundness is proved once in Lean; each claim is then the paper's tight lower bound written
   in that language, the paper's partition as data, and one `decide +kernel`. Estimated ≈ 0.5–1 CPU-h
   for Case J and ≈ 2–4 CPU-h for everything. It follows the paper (same lower bounds, same
   partitions); the extra cost is the one-time development of the checker and its soundness proof.
4. **Mixing** is natural: $J$, $J'$, $w_0$, $w_1$, powers at grid points via the series lemmas
   (M1 or M2) as tables; rectangles via the checker (M2).

Caveats: linear extrapolation from 40-sample tests; M1 glue cost estimated from the (7.3) test
(23 rows) only; kernel time may grow faster than linearly with larger numbers or bigger files;
[DIRX] additionally needs $w_1$, $x_1$ enclosures (same method, negligible cost).
